Your AirPods case
is a mouse.

The sensors are already in there.

AirPods carry a gyroscope and an accelerometer for spatial audio. PodMouse puts them to work.

Three moves and it knows your hand.

Hold still, sweep side to side, then up and down. It learns how your wrist really moves.

A steady hand isn’t required.

Calibration learns your wrist, so the pointer goes where you meant and shake stays out of it.

Gestures

Everything a mouse does.
With nothing on the desk.

Pause and tap

Click. It lands where you were aiming, and moving around never clicks by accident.

Twist and back

Right click. A quick turn of the wrist, there and back, opens the menu.

Twist the other way

Drag, if you turn it on. Pick something up, move it, twist again to drop it.

Two ways to point

In your hand.
Or hands-free.

Case in hand

Open the lid and hold the case like a remote. Great from the couch or in a presentation. Needs Automatic Ear Detection turned off.

In your ears

Turn your head to point. Nod or press the stem to click, shake to pause. No hands at all.

Built right

Small, native,
and none of your business.

No network.

PodMouse never connects to the internet. There is no account, no analytics, no updater phoning home.

No camera.

Motion only, processed in memory and thrown away.

Open source.

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT license.

Native Swift.

A menu bar app that stays out of the way. Calibration and status appear in the notch, then disappear.

Free. For every Mac
with the right AirPods.

Download for Mac

Requires macOS 15 or later and AirPods with head tracking. Tested with AirPods 4.

Case mode needs the lid open and Automatic Ear Detection turned off in your AirPods settings.

If macOS can’t verify the developer, open System Settings › Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway.
